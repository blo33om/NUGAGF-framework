## 1\. Purpose

This Policy establishes how \[Institution Name] governs the use of Generative Artificial Intelligence (GenAI) tools, such as ChatGPT, Gemini, and Copilot, in academic work. It exists to protect academic integrity while enabling responsible, transparent use of GenAI as a learning aid, and to ensure the institution's practices comply with the Nigeria Data Protection Act (NDPA) 2023 and its General Application and Implementation Directive (GAID) 2025.

## 2\. Scope

This Policy applies to all students, faculty, and staff involved in the creation, submission, or assessment of academic work, including assignments, take-home examinations, projects, theses, and research outputs. It does not cover GenAI use in purely administrative operations, which is addressed separately.

## 3\. Definitions

* **Generative Artificial Intelligence (GenAI):** Software capable of producing new text, code, images, or other content in response to a prompt, including but not limited to large language models such as ChatGPT, Gemini, Copilot, and Claude.
* **Disclosure:** A written statement accompanying a submission that identifies which GenAI tool was used, for what specific purpose, and to what extent.
* **Substantial Use:** Use of GenAI that materially shapes the ideas, argument, structure, or original wording of a submission, as distinct from incidental use (e.g., grammar-checking a sentence already written by the student).
* **Undisclosed Substantial Use:** Submission of GenAI-generated or GenAI-substantially-assisted work as one's own original output without the disclosure required under Section 6.
* **Academic Work:** Any assignment, examination, project, thesis, dissertation, laboratory report, or research output submitted for assessment or academic credit.
* **Process Evidence:** Documentation demonstrating the development of a piece of work over time, such as drafts, version history, research notes, or annotated outlines.

## 4\. Guiding Principles

* **Governance over detection.** This institution does not rely solely on AI-text detection software to determine academic misconduct, in recognition of documented reliability limitations in such tools.
* **Education before punishment.** Where appropriate, first instances of unclear or undisclosed GenAI use are treated as a teaching opportunity, not an automatic disciplinary matter.
* **Transparency.** Students and staff must always be informed of how their submitted work may be reviewed or processed.
* **Data protection by design.** Any process that stores, analyses, or transmits student work must comply with the NDPA 2023.
* **Proportionality.** Responses to suspected misuse must be proportionate to the severity and intent involved, not applied uniformly regardless of context.

## 5\. Categories of Use, with Worked Examples

|Category|Description|Status|
|-|-|-|
|Permitted Use|Brainstorming, grammar/language support, code-debugging assistance, understanding concepts|Allowed; disclosure encouraged but not mandatory|
|Conditional Use|Drafting support, literature summarization, generating practice questions|Allowed only where explicitly authorized by the course lecturer, with disclosure required|
|Prohibited Use|Submitting AI-generated content as one's own original work without disclosure; using GenAI during closed-book/invigilated assessments unless expressly permitted|Not permitted|

**Worked examples:**

1. *A student uses ChatGPT to check grammar and punctuation in an already-written essay, making no substantive changes to content.* — This is **Permitted Use**. No disclosure is required, though it is encouraged as good practice.
2. *A student asks ChatGPT to summarize three journal articles to help them decide which to read in full, then writes their own literature review independently.* — This is **Conditional Use**, permitted if the lecturer has authorized research-support tools for that assignment, and must be disclosed.
3. *A student generates a full draft essay from ChatGPT, lightly edits the wording, and submits it as their take-home assignment without any disclosure statement.* — This is **Undisclosed Substantial Use** and falls under **Prohibited Use**. It is treated as a breach of this Policy under Section 8.
4. *A student uses ChatGPT during a supervised, closed-book examination without express permission.* — This is a **Prohibited Use** and is treated as examination misconduct under the institution's existing Examination Regulations, in addition to this Policy.

## 6\. Disclosure Requirement

Where GenAI use is permitted or conditional, students must include a brief AI-use statement with their submission, indicating which tool was used and for what purpose (e.g., "ChatGPT was used to check grammar in Sections 2 and 3"). Disclosure statements are not penalized; they are a normal part of transparent academic practice under this Policy.

## 7\. Assessment Design Responsibilities

Faculties are responsible for classifying their assessments using the Assessment Risk Mapping Tool (see `ASSESSMENT-RISK-MAPPING.md`) and adjusting assessment design where high GenAI-exposure risk is identified — for example, incorporating oral defense components, in-class drafting stages, or process-based submissions (outlines, drafts, version history).

## 8\. Evidence, Investigation, and Sanctions Procedure

### 8.1 Layered Evidence Approach

Where GenAI misuse is suspected, the following layered evidence approach applies, in this order of weight:

1. **Process-based evidence** — absence of drafts, version history, or research trail.
2. **Oral defence/viva** — the student's ability to explain and defend their submitted work.
3. **Contextual inconsistency** — stylistic or factual inconsistency with the student's known work.
4. **AI-detection tool output** — used only as a weak, corroborating signal, never as sole proof of misconduct.

No disciplinary action shall be based solely on an AI-detection tool score.

### 8.2 Sanctions Framework

Sanctions are proportionate to severity and prior history, as follows:

|Level|Circumstance|Response|
|-|-|-|
|Level 1|First instance; minor or ambiguous undisclosed use; student cooperates and demonstrates understanding at oral follow-up|Educational intervention: mandatory disclosure training; resubmission opportunity with disclosure; no mark penalty or formal record|
|Level 2|Clear undisclosed substantial use; first formal offence; or failure to satisfactorily explain submitted work at oral follow-up|Mark penalty on the affected assessment (as determined by Faculty Board policy); formal record entered; referral to Departmental Academic Integrity Officer|
|Level 3|Repeat offence; deliberate concealment (e.g., falsified process evidence); or use during invigilated assessment|Formal misconduct hearing under the institution's Student Disciplinary Regulations; possible zero mark, course failure, or suspension, as determined by the hearing panel|

### 8.3 Investigation Steps

1. Concern is raised by a lecturer, examiner, or automated corroborating signal.
2. Departmental Academic Integrity Officer reviews process evidence and, where necessary, convenes an oral follow-up with the student.
3. Outcome is classified against the Sanctions Framework (Section 8.2) and recorded.
4. Level 2 and Level 3 outcomes are reported to the AI Governance Committee as part of its semester reporting cycle (see `COMMITTEE-TOR.md`).

## 9\. Appeals

A student found in breach of this Policy at Level 2 or Level 3 has the right to appeal the decision through the institution's existing Student Academic Appeals procedure. An appeal must be lodged in writing within fourteen (14) working days of notification of the outcome and must specify the grounds for appeal (e.g., procedural irregularity, new evidence, or disproportionate sanction). Appeals are heard by a panel independent of the original investigating officer. Where an AI-detection tool score formed any part of the original decision, the appeal panel shall disregard that score as determinative evidence, consistent with Section 8.1.

## 10\. Data Protection Compliance

Any tool, vendor, or process used to review, store, or analyse student submissions under this Policy must:

* Have a documented lawful basis for processing under the NDPA 2023;
* Be covered by a Data Processing Agreement where a third-party vendor is involved;
* Be disclosed to students in advance; and
* Undergo an impact assessment before deployment where high-risk processing is involved (e.g., biometric verification).

See `NDPA-COMPLIANCE-CHECKLIST.md` for the full checklist.

## 11\. Roles and Responsibilities

|Role|Responsibility|
|-|-|
|AI Governance Committee|Owns this Policy, reviews it annually, hears escalated cases and semester reports|
|Departmental Academic Integrity Officer|Conducts first-line investigations and oral follow-ups; classifies outcomes under Section 8.2|
|Faculty/Departmental Boards|Classify assessment risk; adapt assessment design; set mark-penalty guidance for Level 2 outcomes|
|Lecturers|Communicate permitted/prohibited use per course; review disclosure statements; raise concerns|
|Students|Comply with disclosure requirements; seek clarification where unsure; may exercise appeal rights under Section 9|
|Data Protection Officer|Approves any tool or vendor involving student data processing|

## 12\. Training

The institution shall provide GenAI-use training to staff and students at the start of each academic session, covering this Policy, disclosure expectations, the sanctions framework, and responsible use practices.

## 13\. Related Policies

This Policy operates alongside, and does not replace, the institution's existing:

* Examination Regulations and Examination Misconduct Policy;
* Plagiarism and Academic Integrity Policy;
* Student Disciplinary Regulations;
* Student Academic Appeals Procedure; and
* Data Protection Policy (issued under the NDPA 2023).

Where a conflict arises between this Policy and an existing regulation, the more specific provision (e.g., an Examination Regulation addressing conduct during invigilated assessments) shall take precedence for that specific circumstance, and the matter shall be referred to the AI Governance Committee for clarification.

## 14\. Review

This Policy shall be reviewed annually by the AI Governance Committee, or sooner if there is a material change in NDPC guidance, national regulation, or significant institutional experience warranting revision.
